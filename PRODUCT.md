# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Convenio serves individuals, teams, and operations staff who publish availability, negotiate a workable time, and keep a reliable record of the meeting. They may arrive focused, hurried, or uncertain about time zones and calendar conflicts. The interface must make the next decision obvious without making scheduling feel transactional or impersonal.

## Product Purpose

Convenio is scheduling software built around one fact: a meeting is an agreement between people. It lets a host state the terms, lets another party choose a time both calendars can keep, and carries that agreement through reminders, changes, and completion. Success means both parties understand what has been agreed, what happens next, and where the record lives.

## Brand Personality

Technical. Bilateral. Decisive.

The voice is manifesto-grade and declarative. Sentences are short, present-tense, and specific. Convenio does not sell speed as an abstract virtue. It gives meetings weight without making ordinary product work ceremonial.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS surfaces that could belong to any productivity tool.
- Cal.diy naming, copy, illustrations, and residual links presented as Convenio.
- Accent-led branding, gradients, decorative red, and color used without semantic meaning.
- Literal yin-yang symbols, handshakes, treaties, or repeated agreement metaphors used as decoration.
- Editorial-serif styling, glassmorphism, identical card grids, nested cards, and shadows used to manufacture hierarchy.
- Marketing language such as "unlock," "empower," "leverage," "seamless," and "streamline."

## Design Principles

1. **Make the agreement visible.** Separate the parties, terms, and choices, then resolve them through one clear action or confirmed state.
2. **Mirror, do not dim.** Light and dark themes are equal black-on-white and white-on-black systems.
3. **Hierarchy before decoration.** Type, spacing, contrast, and grouping establish importance. The converging-arcs mark appears only at meaningful entry, decision, or completion moments.
4. **Keep the product familiar.** Standard scheduling, form, navigation, and dialog affordances remain predictable. Brand expression must never slow a task.
5. **Design every state.** Empty, loading, error, success, disabled, hover, focus, mobile, and reduced-motion states are part of the feature.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across public and authenticated surfaces. Every action must be keyboard operable, every focus state visible, every field programmatically labelled, and every status communicated without color alone. Respect reduced motion, zoom, dynamic copy length, localization, and mobile touch targets of at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels where practical.
